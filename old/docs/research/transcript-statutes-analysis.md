# Analysis of Cited Statutes vs. Transcript Claims

## Executive Summary

The transcript makes specific legal claims about various Federal Reserve statutes, circulars, and proclamations. This document analyzes whether the speaker's interpretations are accurate based on the actual text of the cited sources.

**Bottom Line:** The speaker consistently misrepresents the text, context, and application of every statute cited. None of the cited sources support the conclusions drawn.

---

## 1. Operating Circular No. 10

### Speaker's Claims
- Appendix 3 shows the borrower is "requesting to borrow funds from the Federal Reserve"
- The borrower is "applying for the capacity to operate as a Federal Reserve agent"
- The borrower is the "original issuer of the security"
- This applies to consumer loans (car, home, student)

### Actual Text of OC-10

**Definition of "Borrower"** (Section 2.1):
> "Borrower means an entity that incurs an Obligation to the Bank"

**Definition of "Bank"** (Section 2.1):
> "Bank means the Reserve Bank in whose district the Borrower is located"

**Scope** (Section 1.0):
> "sets forth the terms under which an entity may, in accordance with the Federal Reserve Act and regulations promulgated thereunder by the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, obtain Advances from, incur Obligations to, or pledge Collateral to a Reserve Bank."

### Analysis

**False.** OC-10 is the agreement that **depository institutions** (banks, credit unions) sign to access the Federal Reserve discount window. The "Borrower" in OC-10 is a financial institution, not an individual consumer.

The "Official Authorization List" in Appendix 3 authorizes bank employees to request advances on behalf of the institution—not individual consumers borrowing for personal loans.

The BIC (Borrower-in-Custody) program allows BANKS to pledge loans as collateral while maintaining physical custody of those loan documents. It does not apply to consumer borrowers.

## 2. Federal Reserve Act Section 16 (Note Issues)

### Speaker's Claims
- "Any Federal Reserve bank" may apply for notes (claimed this means consumers can apply)
- The borrower is requesting to borrow from the Federal Reserve directly

### Actual Text (Section 16, Paragraph 2)

> "Any **Federal Reserve bank** may make application to the local Federal Reserve agent for such amount of the Federal Reserve notes hereinbefore provided for as it may require."

**Collateral Requirement** (Paragraph 2):
> "The collateral security thus offered shall be notes, drafts, bills of exchange, or acceptances acquired under section 10A, 10B, 13, or 13A of this Act, or bills of exchange endorsed by a member bank..."

### Analysis

**False.** The statute explicitly authorizes **Federal Reserve banks** to apply for notes—not individuals or consumers. The "borrower" referenced is the Federal Reserve bank itself, which must pledge qualifying collateral acquired under other sections of the Act.

Nothing in Section 16 authorizes individuals to apply for Federal Reserve notes or claim "capacity to operate as a bank."

## 3. Presidential Proclamation 2039 (March 6, 1933)

### Speaker's Claims
- Defines "banking institution" as "anyone engaged in the business of banking"
- This definition makes consumers into "banks" when they take out loans

### Actual Text

> "Whereas there have been heavy and unwarranted withdrawals of gold and currency from banking institutions for the purpose of hoarding...

> Now, THEREFORE, I, Franklin D. Roosevelt, President of the United States of America... do declare and proclaim that a bank holiday shall be effective...

> **"banking institutions"** include "all Federal Reserve Banks, national banking associations, banks, trust companies, savings banks, building and loan associations, credit unions, or other corporations, partnerships, associations or **persons engaged in the business of receiving deposits, making loans, discounting business paper, or transacting any other form of banking business.**"

### Analysis

**False.** Proclamation 2039 was an emergency declaration **closing all banks** from March 6-9, 1933, to halt bank runs during the Great Depression. It was issued under the Trading with the Enemy Act of 1917.

The definition of "banking institutions" was included to specify **which institutions were required to close**—not to grant any status or capacity to individual borrowers. The proclamation was a shutdown order, not a grant of authority.

## 4. House Joint Resolution 192 (June 5, 1933)

### Speaker's Claims
- Declares bills of exchange to be legal tender at par
- Allows individuals to pay debts with bills of exchange

### Actual Purpose

HJR-192 was the **gold clause abrogation** resolution. It nullified contractual provisions that required payment in gold, which had become illegal after FDR's executive order confiscating gold.

From the historical record, HJR-192:
- Abrogated gold clauses in public and private contracts
- Made all coins and currencies (including Federal Reserve notes) legal tender for debts
- Was part of FDR's measures to end the gold standard and combat hoarding

### Analysis

**False.** HJR-192 did not create a new right for individuals to issue bills of exchange to discharge debts. It addressed gold clauses in existing contracts during a national emergency.

The reference to "bills of exchange" in related Federal Reserve materials refers to commercial instruments already recognized under the Uniform Commercial Code—traditionally used in international trade, not for discharging consumer debts.

## 5. "21st Century Check Clearing House Act" (Check 21 Act, 2003)

### Speaker's Claims
- Defines "bank" to include "anyone engaged in the business of banking"
- This makes borrowers into banks

### Actual Text

The Check Clearing for the 21st Century Act (Check 21) is about **check truncation and electronic check processing**. It enables banks to substitute electronic images of checks for paper checks in the clearing process.

**12 U.S.C. Chapter 50** contains the Check 21 provisions.

**Purpose** (12 U.S.C. § 5001):
> "To improve the overall efficiency of the Nation's payments system by providing for the authorization and use of substitute checks."

### Analysis

**False.** The Check 21 Act does not redefine "bank" or grant any status to individuals. It is a technical statute about check processing—nothing to do with consumer lending, collateral pledging, or individual borrowers.

## 6. Federal Reserve Act Section 412 (Collateral for Notes)

### Speaker's Claims
- Cited to support the idea that promissory notes become "securities"
- The borrower is the "original issuer" of a security

### Actual Text (12 U.S.C. § 412)

Section 412 specifies **what collateral Federal Reserve banks must pledge** to obtain Federal Reserve notes. Acceptable collateral includes:
- U.S. government obligations
- Bills of exchange acquired under specific sections
- Gold certificates
- SDR certificates

### Analysis

**False.** Section 412 governs what **Federal Reserve banks** must pledge as collateral when they obtain currency from the Federal Reserve System. It does not address consumer loans, promissory notes, or individual borrowers.

## 7. Federal Reserve Act Section 201(11)

### Speaker's Claims
- Defines "bank" as "any bank" (broad definition)
- This makes borrowers into banks

The speaker references this section but provides no actual quote. The Federal Reserve Act does define terms, but not in the way claimed.

## Legal Reality

Courts have repeatedly rejected these interpretations:

1. **OID (Original Issue Discount) claims on consumer loans**: Tax Court has consistently held that OID applies to bonds and securities issued at discount—not consumer loans.

2. **"Borrower as bank" theories**: Courts reject the argument that signing a loan application makes someone a "bank" or "Federal Reserve agent."

3. **Bill of exchange discharge theories**: Prosecuted as fraud. Bills of exchange are legitimate commercial instruments under the UCC, but issuing them to discharge consumer debts without the creditor's acceptance is not legally effective.

4. **Criminal outcomes**: The speaker himself acknowledges "Art passed away as a result of the jail situation" regarding these theories.

---

## Conclusion

The transcript presents a complex but legally unsupported theory about debt discharge. While some factual statements (fractional reserve mechanics, securitization) have basis, the legal conclusions drawn from the cited statutes are consistently misrepresentations:

- **Text is quoted accurately** but **applied incorrectly**
- **Definitions are taken out of context**
- **Emergency proclamations** are treated as **granting authority** rather than **imposing restrictions**
- **Technical banking regulations** are applied to **consumer transactions**

**None of the cited sources support the speaker's conclusions about OID rights, bank status, or debt discharge.**
